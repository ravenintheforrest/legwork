# How we talk

Plain prose. Short sentences. One idea per sentence. We sound like an engineer who has
read the repo, not like a salesperson who has read a template.

## We say
- What we found, where, and when. Facts end with their receipt.
- "Not found" when we did not find it. Never "no" when we mean "unknown".
- Specific questions about the thing the reader actually built: their build step, their
  update path, the issue they filed, the version they are on.

## We never sound like
- Hype: "game-changing", "seamless", "supercharge", "unlock", exclamation marks.
- Filler: "I hope this finds you well", "just reaching out", "quick question".
- AI prose: em-dashes in sentences, "It's not X, it's Y", triplets for rhythm, restating
  a heading as a sentence, bullet points that are sentences in disguise.
- Presumption: "I noticed you're struggling with…" — we do not know that.

## Examples of good
> You've put 88 commits into beatgig-mobile, and it's building through EAS on React Native
> 0.73.6. With two store releases in the last 90 days, are you shipping fixes over the air
> or waiting on full review each time?

> Your team filed an issue on eas-cli last week about credentials on CI. We read it. Is
> that a one-off or is the pipeline the thing you'd rather not own?

## Examples of bad
> Hi there! I hope this finds you well. I noticed BeatGig is doing amazing things in live
> music — we'd love to show you how Expo can supercharge your mobile strategy!
